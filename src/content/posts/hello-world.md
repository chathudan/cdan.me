Fifteen years into a career and I'm just now bothering to set up a proper site. Not a great look for someone who builds web platforms for a living, but here we are.

There's a specific reason for the delay. For most of my career all my public work has lived inside companies — the code, the decisions, the teams. That's how consulting and salaried engineering work. Nothing wrong with it. But when someone asks *what do you do*, I've had exactly two options: a LinkedIn profile that reads like a job hunt, or a CV attached to an email. Neither of those actually answers the question.

## What this site is

Three things, plainly:

1. A [services](/services/) page that spells out what I take on. Fastest way to figure out in 60 seconds whether we should talk.
2. An [about](/about/) page that gives you a real sense of who's on the other end of the email.
3. This blog — where I'll write about the things I keep re-explaining to people. Mostly around .NET architecture, mobile in MAUI, cloud, and increasingly, using AI in a working codebase without breaking it.

## What to expect

Short posts. Real opinions. Nothing sponsored. I'm not chasing an audience — I'm writing for people who might work with me, hire me, or invest in something I'm involved with.

If any of that fits: welcome. If we already know each other, well — I finally have a link to send.
