Not every problem deserves a proposal, a statement of work, and three weeks of scoping. Sometimes an API is falling over, a build won't sign, a server needs moving, or you have a list of eleven small things nobody on your team has time for. Pay as you go is for that: you buy hours, I work through the list, you see exactly where the time went.

It isn't a separate service. It's a way to buy any of the work on this site — backend and web, mobile, workflow automation, hosting and DevOps — without committing to a project up front.

## What the hours can go on

- **Backend &amp; APIs** — new endpoints, integrations, performance problems, a database that's gotten slow, .NET upgrades that keep getting deferred.
- **Web apps &amp; websites** — features, fixes, accessibility and performance work, or standing up a new site from scratch.
- **Mobile** — .NET MAUI and cross-platform builds, store submissions, the signing and provisioning problems that eat a junior's whole week.
- **Hosting &amp; DevOps** — Linux and Nginx, TLS and Let's Encrypt, Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD pipelines, monitoring and alerting, cloud migrations, and the unglamorous work of making deploys boring.
- **Workflow automation** — the scripts and integrations that remove a recurring manual job.
- **A second opinion** — an hour or two on a design you're unsure about is usually the cheapest hour you'll spend all quarter.

## How it works

1. **You send the list.** An email describing what you need is enough. No brief template, no discovery workshop.
2. **I give you an honest estimate** before starting — including when the honest estimate is "this isn't worth paying me to do."
3. **I work, and you get a log.** Every invoice itemises what was done and how long it took. Time is tracked in 15-minute increments after the first hour.
4. **You stop whenever you want.** Prepaid hours stay valid for 12 months. If you never use them, I refund the balance.

## Good fits

- A live product with no in-house engineer, needing someone reachable when something breaks.
- A team at capacity with a backlog of small, real, unglamorous work.
- A founder who wants to start something small and see how it goes before signing up for a project.
- An existing system you inherited and don't yet trust — a few hours of someone reading it properly is worth more than guessing.

## The honest bits

I'm one person in Melbourne, not a support desk. I work Australian business hours and aim to acknowledge anything urgent within one business day — but I don't offer a 24/7 pager, and I'd rather say that plainly than sell you an SLA I'd break. If you need round-the-clock cover, you need a team, and I'll tell you so.

Pay as you go also isn't the cheapest way to buy a large, well-defined project. If you already know what you want built, one of the [fixed-price packages](/pricing/) will almost certainly cost you less.
